Automatic worktree retention and disk-pressure reclamation

Outcome: Jarvis reclaims storage from completed engineering worktrees automatically, before disk pressure becomes an operational incident. Cleanup is a normal consequence of work lifecycle and storage pressure, not a recurring Operator chore.

Observed baseline

On 2026-11-28 the checkout had 972 registered worktrees and about 6.6 GB free on a 1.8 TB filesystem. A conservative Operator-requested cleanup removed 369 stale worktrees that were clean, integrated, unlocked, old, and contained only reproducible generated files; approximately 68.8 GB was recovered, leaving 603 registered worktrees and about 71–75 GB free. Recent work, unmerged branches, dirty/locked worktrees, and unique ignored evidence such as TestResults and artifacts were retained.

  • Inputs must represent: attempt and all engineering jobs terminal; no active lease/process; clean and unlocked; integrated against a freshly resolved target; no unique untracked/ignored evidence; age; estimated reclaimable bytes; and explicit unknown/ambiguous states. Unknown or missing safety facts are ineligible, with a specific skip reason.
  • Model configurable low/high disk-pressure thresholds with hysteresis. Select the oldest eligible worktrees first with deterministic tie-breaking, stop at a configured healthy free-space target, and avoid repeated churn around one threshold.

Phase B — runtime integration (only after the P0 guard is Accepted and integrated)

  • Wire event-driven eligibility checks after owning attempts and all engineering jobs become terminal, plus a bounded periodic disk-pressure check. Keep the cleanup cadence independent of Scheduler activation.
  • Use only canonical Jarvis inspect/remove worktree operations and their target/concurrency fencing. Re-resolve the integration target immediately before eligibility; preserve branches/commits; never use raw git worktree remove as an automation bypass.
  • Reclaim only clean, unlocked, integrated, inactive worktrees with no unique untracked/ignored evidence. Preserve recent, dirty, unmerged, locked, active, detached/ambiguous, and evidence-bearing worktrees. Never force-remove or delete branches.
  • Inspection/removal must not self-block, while still excluding unrelated jobs and preventing new target use during the reservation. Ordinary ledger-only coordination must remain non-blocking.
  • Expose actual bytes reclaimed, worktrees removed, and skip counts/reasons. Use durable evidence archival only when it is verified before source removal; otherwise retain the worktree.
  • No per-worktree Operator prompts once an explicit retention policy is authorized. No production deletion-policy activation until qualification and independent review are complete.
