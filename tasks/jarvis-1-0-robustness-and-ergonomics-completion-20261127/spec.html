Operator decision — context efficiency as a primary engineering objective, 2026-11-27

Optimize useful completed work per unit of inference context, cost and latency, not merely staying within a generous context window. Context efficiency is a permanent cross-version quality attribute alongside correctness, maintainability and security. Measure representative end-to-end workflows (bootstrap, repeated continuation, orchestration, review, recovery): input/output tokens where provider telemetry is available, otherwise clearly labeled byte/token proxies; tool invocations, repeated payload share, inference turns, elapsed time and completion/correctness. Record realistic baseline and before/after evidence; account for cache hits versus uncached input and output where supported, and provider-specific pricing only when known. Measure total work across agents, not local prompt savings that increase retries, delegation or calls. Establish justified per-surface budgets and regression thresholds from observed distributions and required decision evidence, including skewed histories and large active frontiers. No arbitrary token cap, silent evidence loss, weakened independent review, or metric gaming. Prefer deterministic runtime decisions, purpose-scoped projections, progressive disclosure, immutable references, stable reusable prompt prefixes and concise instructions. Avoid sending complete tool catalogs or schemas when role-scoped exposure is sufficient; authorization remains independent. Preserve mandatory first-turn context and exact evidence access. Treat significant context growth in new/changed routes and instruction templates as a reviewable regression; require justification and measurement. Lead shall incorporate these criteria into canonical architecture/development and review guidance through the existing pertinence task and 1.0 completion work; carry them forward into 2.0 without a second instruction framework.
