Acceptance — demonstrate operational automation, not merely prototypes

  • Scheduler: a non-agent, deterministic, durable authority selects eligible work, admits compatible sessions under actual account/project capacity, reacts to readiness/dependency changes and normal fallback wakeups, launches once with stable provenance, observes registration/continuation, and handles session stalls, terminal results, restart and uncertain submissions without duplicate effects. It must support ordinary autonomous work without a periodic model Manager as a necessary dispatcher. Preserve explicit safety gates and an inspectable recovery path.
  • Challenges: first-class durable conditions with ownership, evidence, resolution, blocking, reopening/cancellation, optional subscription and operator input. Agents can explicitly block on challenge dependencies, release their execution slot, and become safely Ready when all required conditions are satisfied; nonblocked agents discover results normally. The operator can answer through a usable client and the answer is durable. No notification-delivery dependency or fabricated implicit approval.
  • Integration: prove the complete loop: an agent runs, encounters a real dependency, creates a challenge and optionally blocks; an authorized agent or operator resolves it; the dependency becomes eligible; Scheduler admits continuation under capacity and safety rules; completion/checkpoint and observed effects remain durable. Prove cross-project, restart, competing clients, duplicate requests, and unresolved/unknown-effect behavior.
  • Delivery: accepted review and integration on exact source, relevant .NET 11 Release and end-to-end tests, operational documentation and evidence from an actual configured deployment when separately authorized. A Closed task, a default-off fixture, or a passing isolated test is not sufficient to claim full feature completion.
