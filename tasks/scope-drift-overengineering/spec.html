Investigate and prevent runaway scope drift and overengineering in agent work

Problem: Agents can take a bounded engineering task, infer increasingly remote safety or correctness prerequisites, and spend large amounts of work proving or implementing those prerequisites without first checking whether they are necessary for the requested outcome. This can be reinforced by repeated self-review and independent reviews until the original task is obscured. The goal is to preserve engineering rigor and autonomous throughput while preventing unstated assumptions from silently redefining the work. This is a familiar requirements-clarity and expectation-alignment problem, not primarily a permissions or operator-approval problem.

Motivating case

Use Jarvis task task-c4460fbd65a244419650fc5ef5669a6f (audited administrative Working-to-Ready correction) as a concrete case study. Its original contract is a version-checked, auditable ledger state transition preserving assignment and checkpoint evidence. The work expanded into native process-tree containment, cgroups, cross-restart descendant-reaping certificates, fleet protocol drain, and an operator-only production safety decision. Trace exactly which steps were genuinely required by the task, which were additional product ambitions or speculative threat models, and where independent review or the Lead could have identified the divergence. Do not prejudge every safety concern as invalid; explain its relevance or irrelevance to the original acceptance criteria.

Instruction-first prevention model (primary deliverable)

Test the following three ordinary role behaviors as the default solution. Do not begin by designing new Jarvis state, permissions, or policy infrastructure.

  1. Worker — flag scope expansion early. Treat the current canonical task description, its intended outcome, and explicit acceptance criteria as the source of truth. An implementing agent should not edit its own task to rationalize drift. When a discovered concern appears to require a new subsystem, a materially stronger invariant, a new platform guarantee, or significantly more work, stop that expansion before investing in it. Raise a concise Challenge to Lead through Jarvis's canonical challenge mechanism: quote the specific original requirement at risk, give a concrete failure scenario, explain why the existing design cannot meet it, offer the smallest feasible remedy, and distinguish a genuine blocker from optional hardening. Continue clearly in-scope work when safe. Do not expand the task unilaterally.
  2. Reviewer — skeptical, task-anchored review. Independently compare the implementation and its claimed blockers to the original intent and the current Lead-revised, versioned task specification, not merely the worker's latest rationale. Explicitly assess both technical correctness and scope fidelity before acceptance. Challenge newly invented assumptions and distinguish must-fix deviations from separate improvements. Reviewing a technically sound solution to an unrequested problem is not sufficient. A review finding does not automatically rewrite the task's requirements.
  3. Lead — resolve the challenge and enforce scope. Evaluate the causal evidence independently, not by counting supporting commits, tests or reviewer endorsements. Reject unproven prerequisites, choose the smallest correct solution, or deliberately revise the canonical task if expansion is justified. Lead is authorized to clarify and revise normal requirements autonomously, recording the reason and preserving prior versions as a reviewable baseline. Ask the operator only when the overall intended outcome, fundamental tradeoff, or priority cannot reasonably be inferred or decided within the delegated project direction; operator sign-off is not required for routine scope refinement. At integration, verify that the reviewed feature matches the current canonical task and its enduring intended outcome, and that substantive deviations were explicitly resolved by Lead rather than silently normalized.

Keep this proportional: trivial internal refactors and locally necessary details do not require a Challenge; substantial new objectives or hard blockers do. A Challenge is a decision point, not an invitation to build an entire second governance process. If a proposed dependency is not necessary for acceptance, record it as a separate follow-up rather than making it a silent completion gate.

Requirements clarity and autonomous decision rights

  • Identify what minimum task information actually prevents misalignment: the problem to solve, intended observable outcome, meaningful boundaries/non-goals and acceptance examples. Avoid over-prescriptive implementation requirements or unnecessary documentation burdens.
  • Explicitly distinguish implementation detail (worker autonomy), material change of requirements or interpretation (Challenge to Lead, Lead may revise canonical task without operator approval), and uncertain strategic/product intent (Lead may consult operator when genuinely necessary).
  • Use canonical versioned spec revisions to keep changes visible. A worker must not quietly rewrite its own success criterion; the separate Lead coordination role may refine the task with a reason and independently test the new scope against the initial goal.
  • When task information is ambiguous, resolve the ambiguity early using reasonable defaults or a targeted Challenge to Lead; do not infer expansive, unrelated guarantees and then spend substantial effort supporting that inference.
  • Reviewer acceptance must establish both implementation correctness and fidelity to the clarified task. Repeated review of a technically impressive implementation must not launder a mistaken underlying premise into a requirement.

Investigation

  • Reconstruct the sequence of task specs, checkpoints, reviews, instructions, changes and escalations. Identify the first material scope increase, who introduced it, how it was justified, and why it was not challenged or explicitly approved.
  • Check a small sample of other Jarvis tasks (both straightforward and genuinely complex) for the same failure mode. Distinguish necessary engineering depth, prerequisite discovery, unrelated adjacent improvements, and self-imposed impossible-to-prove safety gates.
  • Identify the mechanisms that produce scope drift: reinterpreting goals as broader invariants, treating speculative failure modes as blockers, reviewers silently moving the acceptance bar, accumulating additional commitments, losing the user's original intent, and Lead oversight that only checks technical consistency instead of scope fidelity.
  • Compare lightweight interventions using existing canonical task specs, checkpoints, review summaries, and persistent agent/role instructions. Favor behavioral and workflow fixes before proposing new Jarvis tools, states or mandatory paperwork.

Required proposal

  • Define a practical scope-fidelity check: before treating a newly discovered concern as a blocker, articulate the causal link to an explicit user requirement or acceptance criterion, an observable counterexample, and the smallest adequate fix. Otherwise record it as follow-up work, not an implicit prerequisite.
  • Provide a minimal escalation rule for material expansions of scope, architecture or proof burden, using Challenge to Lead first. Lead has delegated authority to resolve and version normal task refinements. Escalate to the operator only for unresolved product goals or strategic tradeoffs, not routine requirement decisions.
  • Define the responsibilities of workers, reviewers and Lead: worker flags changed assumptions; reviewer assesses both correctness and whether findings are in scope; Lead can stop/re-scope runaway work rather than accepting its newly invented conditions.
  • Keep a clear exit criterion: once the original acceptance conditions are met and independently reviewed, the task can finish. Optional hardening, distant edge cases and adjacent features become separately prioritized tasks unless they demonstrably invalidate the requested behavior.
  • Recommend the smallest set of changes, if any, to Jarvis roles/AGENTS.md/task templates. Do not invent a complex new governance subsystem to solve overengineering.

Evidence and acceptance

  1. Concise retrospective of the Worker 3 case with specific source/task/review milestones and at least one earlier intervention point that would have saved effort without sacrificing a real correctness guarantee.
  2. Two or more contrasting task examples demonstrate that the proposal catches unjustified expansion but does not suppress necessary fixes.
  3. Deliver concise, immediately usable persistent instruction wording for workers, reviewers and Lead embodying the three behaviors above. Include a short Challenge-to-Lead example, a task-to-implementation acceptance check, an example of justified expansion, and how Lead revises a canonical specification autonomously without permitting the implementer to redefine its own target.
  4. Test the proposed check retrospectively against the case and apply it to at least one current task in dry-run form. State false-positive/false-negative risks and expected coordination overhead.
  5. Present an explicit recommendation prioritizing role/AGENTS.md instructions over new tooling, and any operator decision required. No automatic bulk edits to agents, roles or project processes and no deployment without approval.

Constraints: Preserve autonomous project delivery and avoid introducing an operator approval bottleneck. The goal is better judgment and throughput, not weaker engineering standards. No Codex through Jarvis. Keep the investigation bounded and focus on preventing a repeat of the same failure mode.
