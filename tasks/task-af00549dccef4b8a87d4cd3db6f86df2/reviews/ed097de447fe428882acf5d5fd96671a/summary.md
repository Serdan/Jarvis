## Ownership and liveness invariants

1. **Active registered sessions and transports are never pruned by time or disconnected quotas.** Disconnected sessions have 7d TTL, 64/user and 2048 global deterministic oldest-first quotas. Disconnected failure reasons remain in `ListDevices` until eviction. Their removal also clears stale defaults and conversations.
2. **Selections bound in two dimensions.** Conversation device pins have 24h *idle* TTL, 256/user and 8192 global caps, with touched timestamps refreshed only when read or set. Deterministic least-recently-used eviction breaks ties by user/conversation ID; each insertion enforces both caps even without scheduled sweep. Default pointer at most one per user and only for a retained device. Session/selection dictionaries and default cleanup share a single UserService lock.
3. **Deterministic fallback and negative space.** Explicit per-call device override never falls back. An unexpired selection of a disconnected device stays disconnected rather than rerouting. After a target expires, automatic selection falls back to the user's default; expired default is rebound to oldest remaining *Registered* session (tie by DeviceId) or cleared. A device reconnecting before TTL retains its pinned selections; after eviction it cannot steal a repaired default or reattach expired conversation pins. No cross-user selection is possible.
4. **Generation and stale response safety.** Each registration receives next monotonic process-local generation. Old generation cannot recur within process after a device record is pruned, without retaining an unbounded tombstone dictionary. SignalR pending tracker also requires authenticated user, connection ID, and correlation ID. On restart, UserService and response tracker are both new; no persisted generations or stale pending callbacks remain. Existing tests now assert generation freshness rather than per-device numeric numbering.
5. **Authenticated transport ownership.** Registration requires a known matching authenticated transport; reused still-active SignalR connection ID is refused instead of overwriting the owner. Rebinding the same device onto a new connection invalidates the old association, and a delayed stale disconnect cannot remove the replacement. A single transport switching devices marks the old device disconnected.
6. **Clock, DI, deployment.** No timer, background process, new persistence layer or protocol change. Every service access performs a cheap interval check; one expiry scan at most every 2m, plus bounded insertion checks. Explicit public `UserService()` constructor resolves through actual `Microsoft.Extensions.DependencyInjection` registration; injected `(clock, retention)` constructor is used only for deterministic tests. This addresses a *real finding*: initial F# optional-argument version compiled but caused real OAuth/readiness server tests to fail because DI saw non-optional `FSharpOption` constructor parameters. Fix is validated by production-style ServiceCollection resolution and the real server startup tests. Aggregate-only `RetentionCounts` and forced `PruneExpired` support diagnostics without exposing identifiers.

## Deliberate limits and cost tradeoffs

- Active registrations/transports are intentionally exempt: counts of actually live connections are bounded by SignalR/server infrastructure, not artificial forced expiry in UserService. Disconnected session and conversation records have both TTL and hard per-user/global quotas.
- Global quotas can displace older records belonging to another user without routing across owners. This is a memory ceiling, not a fairness scheduler. Per-user quotas limit single-user occupancy.
- The 2-minute lazy sweep means expiry is observed at the next maintenance access and may lag TTL by up to the sweep interval during active use or indefinitely while totally idle; insertion caps still bound churn. No need for another housekeeping daemon.
- The feature does not preserve preferences on restart, add a database, or change client wire protocol. No deployment is performed with source merge.
