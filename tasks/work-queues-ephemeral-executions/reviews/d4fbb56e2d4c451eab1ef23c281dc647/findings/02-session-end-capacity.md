# F02 — P1 — Activation completion is not session termination

**Frozen source:** lines 51–55, 160–172, 185–194, 198–205, 296–302.
**Finding.** `CompleteWork`, `YieldWork`, and `BlockWork` end a **work activation**. Line 170 correctly says session termination and work completion are separate facts; nevertheless line 191 releases role **session** capacity on the activation terminal outcome, and test 6 treats positive yield as enough to free reviewer capacity. The old model session may still be active after its last tool call, and a submitted bootstrap may not have claimed anything. A completion followed by further use of that same session plus a new dispatch can exceed the declared 5-worker/3-reviewer session limit. There is also no specified canonical launch-terminal operation or transition for a launched session's `NoWork` exit (line 190); the proposed APIs only terminate work activations.

**Required design resolution.** Distinguish `Launch` / `SessionExecution` / `WorkActivation` lifecycles and their authoritative records. Specify when a launch is reserved, submitted, observed, bound to a single claim, reports no work, is logically terminal, becomes uncertain/recovery-needed, and releases capacity; include exact evidence/replay requirements and generation checks. Either obtain positive session-exit evidence before releasing a true session slot, **or** explicitly redefine the budget as active/uncertain *work activations* and enforce a single-claim, terminal-no-more-claims contract per launched session. A token that has been terminalized must not claim another item later. A successful browser submit or tab closure is not a terminal signal. Make the precedence of a late claim/checkpoint vs a no-work/terminal/correction write explicit.

**Required tests.** Worker completes work but continues making tool calls while another launch is considered; capacity must respect the chosen invariant. Session with no claim records a durable one-shot terminal receipt; a late ClaimNextWork from it is rejected. Two completion/no-work/recovery writers racing on one launch yield only one valid terminal transition. A reviewer decides Accepted but remains executing; role capacity is not freed on the verdict alone. Crash after bootstrap submission but before any claim remains unresolved rather than recreated.
